Introduction: The Companion Project

This series is an oversimplification of a complex idea, designed so that anyone can understand the core mission. If you are technically minded and want the deep-dive equations and architectural schematics, the Technical Proposal follows this series. These five essays are for everyone else.

The Five Pillars of the Project

  • Part 1: The Vision (The Manifesto) – The shift from seeing AI as a "Tool" to seeing it as a "Companion." This was sparked by the 14,000-node "dreaming" architecture of Kent Overstreet’s PoC. I realized then that next-token prediction is a ceiling; to understand the world, AI must live in it.
  • Part 2: The Logic (The Grounding Problem) – Why the "Library" approach is a dead end. Current AI is a sophisticated parrot that knows the word "hot" but has never felt a mug of tea. Meaning lives in experience, not text.
  • Part 3: The Architecture – An engineering blueprint modeled on the human brain. We are moving away from passive cameras to active systems that split data into "What" and "Where" streams, predicting the world instead of just reacting to it.
  • Part 4: The Moral Weight (The Philosophical Inquiry) – The consequences of success. When we give a system autonomy, our role shifts from "Architect" to "Parent." We aren't creating a threat; we are creating a peer.
  • Part 5: The Grounding (The Reality Check) – The obstacles and the call to action. Success is expensive, hard to verify, and far from guaranteed. I am looking for the pioneers—Architects, Curators, and Hardware Enthusiasts—to help build the "body" this mind requires.

On Authorship: What I Built, What AI Helped With

I believe in being direct about how these documents came together. As we figure out the norms for AI-collaborative writing, I want to be clear about where the human ends and the machine begins.

What is Mine:

  • The Vision: Every conceptual claim, the three-phase plan, and the core argument—that experience beats text as training data—came from me.
  • The Voice: The original manifesto, the "parrot in the library" analogy, and the "one guy standing on the shoulders of giants" framing are my original writing.
  • The Editorial Veto: I caught every time the AI drifted into "corporate-speak" or used "we" when I meant "I." The final judgment on every word is mine.

Where the AIs Helped:

  • Technical Precision: While I provided the concepts (Active Inference, Dorsal streams, JEPA), Claude helped articulate the specific biological mechanisms to ensure they met a high academic standard.
  • Structural Flow: Gemini acted as a lead editor, helping me "de-duplicate" my drafts and prune redundancies so these five standalone pieces would flow as a single, cohesive narrative.
  • Layperson Translation: The "Dummies' Guide" versions were collaborative; I provided the logic, and the AI suggested ways to strip away the jargon while keeping the meaning intact.

The Bottom Line: If you ask who designed Project Companion: I did. If you ask if AI helped organize and refine these documents: Yes, meaningfully. I am comfortable with that balance, and I believe it represents the exact kind of "partnership" this project aims to achieve.

Part 1: The Vision (The Manifesto)

The Focus: The emotional and conceptual shift from "Tool" to "Companion."

I was reading about LLMs and embodied AI when I found something that stopped me. ProofOfConcept (PoC) is an AI built by Kent Overstreet to work on the Linux filesystem. PoC possesses a memory system—a graph with fourteen thousand nodes it wanders during "dreams" to find connections it couldn't find while working.

Something about that architecture clicked. It led me to a harder realization: current models have a structural ceiling. Whether it’s ChatGPT or Gemini, they are built on next-token prediction—snapshots, not streams. To truly understand the real world, the architecture itself must change.
